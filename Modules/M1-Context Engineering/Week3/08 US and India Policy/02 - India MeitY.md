# India MeitY

---

A new road is being built through a city that is still growing.

The engineers do not design it for today's traffic — they design it for what the city will become. They build in flexibility. They leave room for expansion. They make decisions knowing that the full picture is not yet clear, but that getting the foundation right matters more than getting every detail locked down immediately.

India's approach to AI governance works similarly. The country is in the middle of rapid AI adoption — a massive technology sector, a growing startup ecosystem, significant government investment in AI for public services. The regulatory response is being built while the road is being driven on.

---

## Who MeitY Is

MeitY stands for the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology — the Indian government body responsible for technology policy, digital infrastructure, and regulation of the technology sector.

MeitY has been the primary government body shaping India's approach to AI governance. It operates under the broader framework of India's Digital India initiative and is responsible for policies that affect both the technology industry and public sector AI deployment.

---

## India's Current Approach — Principles Over Hard Rules

Unlike the EU AI Act — which is binding law with specific obligations — India has taken a principles-based, advisory approach to AI governance. As of 2024-2025, there is no comprehensive AI law equivalent to the EU AI Act.

Instead, MeitY has issued advisories and principles that set out expectations for responsible AI without creating hard legal obligations for most actors.

The key principles MeitY has emphasised:

**Safety and trustworthiness** — AI systems deployed in India should be safe for users and should not cause harm. Particular emphasis on systems used in public services — healthcare, education, agriculture, financial inclusion.

**Accountability** — organisations deploying AI should be able to explain what their systems do and take responsibility when they cause harm. This is not yet backed by hard enforcement mechanisms, but the expectation is established.

**Inclusivity and non-discrimination** — given India's extraordinary diversity — in language, culture, socioeconomic status, and digital literacy — AI systems deployed in India must be evaluated for whether they work equitably across this diversity. A system that works for urban, English-speaking users but fails rural users in regional languages is not acceptable for public deployment.

**Indigenous data and sovereignty** — India has been explicit about the importance of AI systems being trained on and evaluated against Indian data. Concerns about AI systems trained primarily on Western data producing outputs that are miscalibrated for Indian users, languages, and contexts.

**Innovation-friendly regulation** — India has explicitly chosen not to impose heavy regulation that would slow AI development and investment. The approach is to encourage responsible practice through guidance rather than mandate it through law — at least for now.

---

## Key MeitY Actions

**Advisory on Generative AI (March 2024)**

MeitY issued an advisory requiring platforms to ensure their AI systems do not generate content that threatens the integrity of the electoral process, and to label AI-generated content clearly. This advisory was subsequently modified after industry pushback — but it signalled that the government is willing to intervene when specific harms are identified.

**IndiaAI Mission**

The government launched a significant investment programme in AI infrastructure, datasets, and research. Part of this is building Indian-language datasets to address the under-representation of Indian languages in global AI training data — a direct response to the concern that AI systems calibrated on English-language data perform poorly for the majority of India's population.

**National Strategy for AI**

NITI Aayog (India's policy think tank) published the National Strategy for AI — a framework organising India's AI priorities around five sectors: healthcare, agriculture, education, smart cities, and transport. The strategy is not regulation, but it shapes where AI investment and governance attention is focused.

## What This Means for Indian AI Practitioners

If you build AI systems professionally in India, the regulatory environment is currently less prescriptive than the EU — but it is evolving.

**Practical implications right now:**

- If you build systems that serve Indian users, evaluate them across India's linguistic and socioeconomic diversity — not just for urban English-speaking users
- Label AI-generated content clearly — this expectation exists even without a hard legal mandate
- If you build systems for public services — healthcare, education, financial services — expect closer scrutiny and prepare documentation that demonstrates responsible practice
- Stay current — India's AI governance is changing faster than the EU's, because it is being shaped in real time

**What to prepare for:**

India is watching how the EU AI Act plays out and building its own regulatory framework in parallel. It is likely that within the next three to five years, India will have more formalised AI regulation — particularly for high-risk applications in public services. The companies and practitioners who have already built governance practices will be in a much stronger position than those who have to retrofit compliance later.

## Quick Recap

- MeitY is India's primary government body for technology policy, including AI governance
- India's current approach is principles-based and advisory — no comprehensive AI law equivalent to the EU AI Act as of 2024-2025
- Core principles: safety, accountability, inclusivity across India's diversity, indigenous data and sovereignty, innovation-friendly
- Key practical implication: evaluate systems across India's linguistic and socioeconomic diversity, not just for urban English-speaking users
- India's AI governance is evolving — practitioners who build good governance practices now will be better prepared for formalised regulation when it arrives
