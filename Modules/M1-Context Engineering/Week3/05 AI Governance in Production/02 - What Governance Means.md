# What Governance Means

---

Three students are working on the same project.

The first says: "I think we should be honest about what our system cannot do. It feels wrong to let users trust it more than they should." That is ethics — a personal sense of what is right.

The second says: "Let us write a documentation template that records every limitation and gets reviewed before we submit." That is governance — a structured process that turns the ethical intention into a reliable practice.

The third says: "The university's assessment guidelines require us to disclose limitations in our submission." That is regulation — an external rule that mandates certain behaviour regardless of personal intention.

All three are pointing at the same thing: responsible behaviour. But they are doing it at different levels. Understanding the difference matters because they require different responses from you.

---

## Ethics — What You Believe Is Right

Ethics is your personal or professional moral framework. It shapes how you think about your responsibilities as someone building AI systems.

Should this system be honest about its limitations? Should it be designed to protect users who might be vulnerable? Should it avoid producing content that could mislead or harm?

Ethics gives you the motivation and the direction. But ethics alone does not produce reliable outcomes — because it depends on individual judgment, and judgment varies between people, between moments, and between situations under pressure.

A team of five people with good individual ethics can still build a system that causes harm — because each person assumed someone else was handling the governance.

---

## Governance — How You Ensure It Happens

Governance is the set of structures, processes, and controls that turn ethical intentions into reliable practice.

It answers the question: how do we ensure that responsible behaviour happens consistently — not just when individuals remember to think about it, but systematically, every time, even under pressure?

For an AI system, governance includes:
- Defined criteria for what the system should and should not do
- A testing process that verifies the criteria are met
- Documentation that records decisions, limitations, and changes
- A review process before the system is used by real people
- A way to catch and respond to problems after deployment

This is what you have been building across Weeks 2 and 3. The five-role framework, the evaluation harness, the documentation template — these are governance structures. They ensure responsible behaviour reliably, not just when the builder happens to think about it.

---

## Regulation — What You Are Required to Do

Regulation is external rules imposed by governments, standards bodies, or institutions. It creates legal or institutional obligations — things you must do, regardless of your personal ethics or your team's governance practices.

The EU AI Act is regulation. NIST AI RMF is a regulatory framework. University assessment guidelines that require disclosure of AI limitations are regulation at an institutional level.

Regulation sets a floor — the minimum required. Good governance goes beyond the floor. Good ethics motivates going beyond governance.

The relationship is:

```
ETHICS → motivates → GOVERNANCE → implements → behaviour
REGULATION → mandates → minimum behaviour
```

Strong teams have all three. They build systems they are proud of ethically, governed by structures that make pride reliable, and compliant with regulations that protect users legally.

---

## Why This Matters for What You Build

For the systems you build in this course, regulation does not impose legal obligations on you personally — you are students, not companies deploying products at scale.

But understanding the three levels prepares you for what happens after graduation:

- The systems you build professionally will face regulatory scrutiny. Understanding regulation now means you are not learning it under pressure later.
- The governance practices you build now — evaluation harnesses, documentation templates, defined quality criteria — are directly transferable to professional environments.
- The ethical foundation — thinking seriously about who is affected by your system and how — is something you develop through practice, not just through reading about it.

---

## Quick Recap

- Ethics is personal moral judgment — motivates responsible behaviour but does not guarantee it
- Governance is the structured process that turns ethical intentions into reliable practice — evaluation harnesses, documentation, review processes
- Regulation is external rules that mandate minimum behaviour — EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, institutional guidelines
- Strong teams have all three — ethics motivates, governance implements, regulation sets the floor
