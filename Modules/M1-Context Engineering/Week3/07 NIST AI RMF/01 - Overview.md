# NIST AI RMF — Overview

---

The EU AI Act tells you what you must do. The NIST AI Risk Management Framework tells you how to do it.

Think of it this way. A building code says a structure must be able to withstand a certain load. That is the requirement. Engineering practice — how to design foundations, how to calculate load distribution, how to inspect for weaknesses — is how you meet it.

The EU AI Act sets requirements. NIST AI RMF is the engineering practice for meeting them.

---

## What NIST Is

NIST stands for the National Institute of Standards and Technology — a US government body that develops standards, guidelines, and frameworks used across industry and government worldwide.

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF) was published in January 2023. It is voluntary — no law requires you to follow it. But it has become the de facto standard for how organisations structure their AI governance practices. Many companies, governments, and institutions reference it when building AI systems, even outside the United States.

It is practical. It is structured. And it maps directly onto the governance work you have been doing.

---

## The Four Functions

The NIST AI RMF organises AI risk management into four functions. They are designed to work together as a continuous cycle — not a one-time checklist.

**GOVERN** — establishing the organisational structures, policies, and culture that make responsible AI possible.

**MAP** — identifying and understanding the risks specific to an AI system and the context it operates in.

**MEASURE** — assessing, analysing, and tracking those risks using defined metrics and methods.

**MANAGE** — prioritising and responding to identified risks — mitigating, monitoring, and learning from what happens.

```
GOVERN
  ↓
MAP → MEASURE → MANAGE
  ↑________________________|
```

Govern sets the foundation. Map, Measure, and Manage run as a continuous cycle throughout the system's life.

---

## Why This Matters for You

You have been doing all four functions already — without calling them by these names.

When you wrote the five-role framework, you were doing Govern — establishing what the system is and what it should not do.

When you identified the edge cases and adversarial inputs your context package might face, you were doing Map — identifying the specific risks.

When you ran the evaluation harness and calculated a pass rate, you were doing Measure — assessing risk using defined criteria.

When you fixed the constraints that failed, updated the documentation, and re-ran the harness, you were doing Manage — responding to identified risks.

The NIST AI RMF gives a name and a structure to practices you have already been building. Understanding it lets you communicate those practices clearly in professional contexts — and extend them as the systems you build become more complex.

## Quick Recap

- NIST AI RMF is a voluntary framework that provides practical structure for AI risk management — complementing regulatory requirements like the EU AI Act
- It organises AI risk management into four functions: Govern, Map, Measure, Manage
- Govern sets the foundation; Map, Measure, and Manage run as a continuous cycle
- You have been doing all four functions already — the framework gives them structure and professional language
